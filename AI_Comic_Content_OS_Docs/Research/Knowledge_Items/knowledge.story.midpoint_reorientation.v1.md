knowledge_id: knowledge.story.midpoint_reorientation.v1
version: v1
category: story_structure
principle: A central story turn should reorient the protagonist's pursuit by changing the stakes, urgency, relationship between storylines, or meaning of apparent progress.
rationale: Long narratives often sag when the middle only extends prior activity; a consequential reorientation creates a different second movement rather than more of the first.
when_to_use:
  - Reviewing the center of a whole-story arc or a major longform movement.
  - Diagnosing repeated investigation, pursuit, or relationship beats.
when_not_to_use:
  - To force an event at an exact halfway percentage or episode.
  - To require a false victory, false defeat, deadline, and storyline crossing simultaneously.
application_rules:
  - Make the turn alter subsequent strategy or available choices.
  - Connect increased stakes to the character's specific reason for caring, not only a larger external threat.
  - Prefer irreversible action or reinterpretation over an announcement that conditions are now worse.
anti_patterns:
  - The middle adds danger while the protagonist continues the same plan unchanged.
  - A nominal victory has no new cost and produces no different action.
examples:
  - weak: The heroine learns that the conspiracy is larger and continues interviewing witnesses.
  - stronger: Publishing partial evidence protects one witness but identifies her hidden source, forcing her to abandon private investigation for a public legal strategy.
