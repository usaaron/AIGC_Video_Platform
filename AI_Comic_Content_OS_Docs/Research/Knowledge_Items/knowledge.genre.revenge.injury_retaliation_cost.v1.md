category: genre_blueprint
principle: A revenge narrative should connect a legible injury to a chosen act of retaliation and explore the human, moral, or social consequences of pursuing redress.
genre_blueprint:
  audience_expectation: The grievance, chosen retaliation, and consequences remain causally connected.
  emotional_promise: Anticipation of redress complicated by the cost and moral pressure of obtaining it.
  common_story_engine: Injury creates a retaliatory goal; each action provokes resistance, counteraction, or a higher personal cost.
  common_failure_modes: Passive wish fulfillment, unrelated punishment, repetitive humiliation, or retaliation without consequence.
  typical_conflict_patterns: Justice versus vengeance; target versus avenger; retaliation versus collateral harm; truth versus control.
rationale: Revenge drama gains force from the tension between an intelligible demand for justice and the violence, obsession, or reciprocal harm generated by retaliation.
when_to_use:
  - When revenge is the central story engine rather than a background motive.
  - Designing escalation from grievance to irreversible action.
when_not_to_use:
  - To mandate violent punishment.
  - When the story's actual engine is legal investigation, survival, or reconciliation.
application_rules:
  - Establish the perceived injury and why ordinary redress appears unavailable or insufficient.
  - Give the avenger consequential choices about method and limit.
  - Let retaliation change relationships and expose a cost, counterclaim, or cycle.
anti_patterns:
  - Revenge is only a slogan while events are driven by coincidence.
  - Punishment arrives without action, sacrifice, or moral consequence.
examples:
  - weak: The antagonist is defeated by an unrelated accident.
  - stronger: The protagonist exposes the antagonist but sacrifices evidence that could also protect someone innocent.
