knowledge_id: knowledge.storyline.character_driven_parallel_arcs.v1
version: v1
category: story_structure_and_serialization
principle: Parallel storylines should arise from characters with distinct goals and should alter one another through consequential intersections rather than functioning as decorative side plots.
rationale: Character-driven A/B/C stories expand a series only when each line has its own pressure and changes the resources, choices, or meaning of another line.
when_to_use:
  - Designing main, relationship, family, rival, or institutional storylines.
  - Reviewing whether supporting characters serve a dramatic purpose without becoming plot devices.
when_not_to_use:
  - To require three storylines in every episode.
  - To keep a subplot active after it has no independent question or consequence.
application_rules:
  - Assign each active line a character owner, goal, conflict, local resolution, and effect on another line.
  - Plan intersections around choices or consequences, not coincidental meetings.
  - Allow lines to pause, conclude, or change ownership when their function is complete.
anti_patterns:
  - A relationship line only repeats emotional commentary on the main investigation.
  - A supporting character exists solely to deliver a clue and then disappears.
examples:
  - weak: Family conflict supplies hostile conversations between investigation scenes.
  - stronger: A succession struggle changes who controls the archive, forcing both the investigator and antagonist to adopt new strategies.
