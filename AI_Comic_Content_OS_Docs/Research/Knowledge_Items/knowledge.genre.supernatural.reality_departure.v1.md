category: genre_blueprint
principle: Establish at least one meaningful departure from mimetic reality and make the uncertainty, threat, wonder, or symbolic consequence of that departure affect the characters.
genre_blueprint:
  audience_expectation: An extranormal element disturbs ordinary assumptions and matters to the story's choices and consequences.
  emotional_promise: Uncanny uncertainty, fear, wonder, fascination, or symbolic confrontation with what ordinary reality cannot contain.
  common_story_engine: A supernatural presence or rule intrudes on ordinary life, and interpretation of that intrusion changes action and relationship.
  common_failure_modes: Supernatural decoration, inconsistent powers used for convenience, or ordinary conflict unaffected by the premise.
  typical_conflict_patterns: Belief versus disbelief; desire versus taboo; human need versus supernatural rule; intimacy versus monstrous consequence.
rationale: Supernatural fiction gains identity from an impossible or extranormal element that disturbs the assumptions of the represented world.
when_to_use:
  - Ghost, vampire, occult, haunting, or paranormal story elements.
  - Checking whether supernatural content has narrative function.
when_not_to_use:
  - To require a single universal magic system.
  - When the work intentionally explains the apparent supernatural through natural causes.
application_rules:
  - Define what ordinary assumption the element violates.
  - Show how characters interpret and respond to the violation.
  - Maintain enough consistency for consequences to feel earned, while preserving genre-appropriate uncertainty.
anti_patterns:
  - Supernatural imagery never changes choices or stakes.
  - New powers appear only to solve the climax.
examples:
  - weak: A vampire label changes only appearance.
  - stronger: Hunger, secrecy, and immortality create concrete limits on trust and survival between the leads.
