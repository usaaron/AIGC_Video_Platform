knowledge_id: knowledge.story.causal_sequence.v1
version: v1
category: story_structure
principle: Arrange major incidents so later events arise plausibly or necessarily from earlier events, rather than merely occurring after them.
rationale: Causal order produces unity and lets consequences carry narrative momentum.
when_to_use:
  - Building Scene Goal / Conflict / Outcome chains.
  - Evaluating whether a scene can be removed without affecting later events.
when_not_to_use:
  - To eliminate deliberate ambiguity, montage, or associative structures whose organizing logic is clearly established.
  - To require literal dialogue references to prior scenes.
application_rules:
  - Record what state changes after each scene.
  - Make at least one later goal, obstacle, reveal, or cost depend on that changed state.
anti_patterns:
  - Parallel scenes repeat the same conflict without changing available choices.
  - A later scene ignores the consequences of the prior outcome.
examples:
  - weak: The heroine confronts a suspect; the next scene begins an unrelated date.
  - stronger: Her confrontation alerts the suspect, forcing the date to become a covert exchange.
source_reference:
  source_title: Poetics
  source_author_or_organization: Aristotle; translated by S. H. Butcher
  source_url: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1974/1974-h/1974-h.htm
  publication_reference: Project Gutenberg eBook 1974; Parts VIII-IX on unity and probable or necessary sequence.
  source_type: foundational_dramatic_theory
  authority_level: foundational
  extraction_confidence: high
confidence: high
