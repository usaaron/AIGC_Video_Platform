category: genre_blueprint
principle: In genre romance, the developing central relationship should remain a primary plot engine and lead to an emotionally satisfying, optimistic relationship outcome.
genre_blueprint:
  audience_expectation: The central relationship materially drives the main plot and reaches an emotionally satisfying, optimistic state.
  emotional_promise: Emotional justice through mutual risk, struggle, connection, and earned commitment.
  common_story_engine: Two or more people pursue or resist a relationship while internal and external obstacles test whether it can work.
  common_failure_modes: The romance is removable, the leads do not make relationship-shaping choices, or the ending abandons the relationship contract.
  typical_conflict_patterns: Trust versus self-protection; intimacy versus external duty; incompatible needs; social or situational obstacles.
rationale: This is a minimum audience contract identified by Romance Writers of America, not a requirement that every subplot or tone be light.
when_to_use:
  - When Creative Intent explicitly selects genre romance.
  - Checking whether external plot events materially affect the relationship.
when_not_to_use:
  - For stories that merely contain a romantic subplot.
  - For intentionally tragic love stories not presented as genre romance.
application_rules:
  - Keep the lovers' struggle to form or sustain the relationship central to causality.
  - Build emotional justice through reciprocal risk, choice, and changed relationship state.
  - Allow any setting, tone, or sensuality consistent with explicit content constraints.
anti_patterns:
  - The relationship can be removed without changing the main plot.
  - The ending abandons the relationship question without purposeful genre signaling.
examples:
  - weak: Two leads flirt while an unrelated mystery carries every consequential choice.
  - stronger: Solving the mystery requires choices that test whether each lead can trust and protect the relationship.
