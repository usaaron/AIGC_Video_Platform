knowledge_id: knowledge.character.relationship_definition.v1
version: v1
category: character_design
principle: Define important characters relationally, so each person's goals, methods, and values sharpen or challenge those of the others.
rationale: A cast becomes dramatically useful when relationships generate choices and conflict rather than merely adding biographies.
when_to_use:
  - Designing protagonist, counterpart, and antagonist functions.
  - Checking whether supporting characters affect the protagonist's development.
when_not_to_use:
  - To make every minor character a thematic mirror.
  - To erase independent goals in favor of serving the protagonist.
application_rules:
  - Compare what each major character wants and how each pursues it.
  - Give each key relationship a source of alignment and a source of friction.
anti_patterns:
  - Supporting characters exist only to deliver exposition.
  - The antagonist opposes the hero without an intelligible objective.
examples:
  - weak: Both leads want justice and use identical methods.
  - stronger: Both want justice, but one relies on public truth while the other relies on private control.
