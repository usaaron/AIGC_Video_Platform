knowledge_id: knowledge.character.motivation_interrelationship.v1
version: v1
category: character_design
principle: Ground behavior in intelligible motives and relationships; conflict should develop from what people believe, want, and do to one another.
rationale: Motivation and interrelationship make behavior legible without reducing characters to plot devices.
when_to_use:
  - Building antagonist logic and relationship conflict.
  - Reviewing whether an action is consistent with established psychology.
when_not_to_use:
  - To require characters to explain their motives aloud.
  - To assume behavior must be fully rational or morally justified.
application_rules:
  - Record the motive that makes an action plausible to the character.
  - Use desire, fear, belief, and internal contradiction only when they predict or complicate observable choices.
  - Express motive through conduct and relational pressure before exposition.
anti_patterns:
  - A villain acts only because the plot needs obstruction.
  - A profile lists fears and beliefs that never constrain behavior.
  - A character reverses values without pressure, discovery, or consequence.
examples:
  - weak: He betrays her because he is evil.
  - stronger: He betrays her because preserving institutional control matters more to him than personal loyalty.
