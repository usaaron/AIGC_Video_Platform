---
knowledge_id: knowledge.reveal.prepared_surprise.v1
version: v1
category: conflict_and_emotion
principle: A reveal should surprise by changing the meaning of established evidence, not by replacing the story with previously unavailable facts.
rationale: Prepared surprise preserves both suspense and retrospective coherence.
when_to_use:
  - Mystery reveals, hidden identities, betrayals, and cliffhangers.
  - Reviewing whether a twist is earned.
when_not_to_use:
  - When the intended effect is surreal rupture rather than causal revelation.
  - To make every clue conspicuous enough to reveal the answer immediately.
application_rules:
  - Plant at least one fair observable precursor.
  - Make the reveal alter a relationship, choice, or risk rather than only explain backstory.
anti_patterns:
  - A new object or relative appears solely to justify the twist.
  - The reveal confirms the current plan and changes no action.
examples:
  - weak: A secret twin appears without prior trace.
  - stronger: A recurring access anomaly is revealed to be the protagonist's ally using a hidden identity.
source_reference:
  source_title: Poetics
  source_author_or_organization: Aristotle; translated by S. H. Butcher

  publication_reference: Project Gutenberg eBook 1974; Parts X-XI on reversal and recognition arising from plot.
  source_type: foundational_dramatic_theory
  authority_level: foundational

confidence: high
