---
knowledge_id: knowledge.visual.observable_action.v1
version: v1
category: visual_narrative
principle: Describe what can be seen or heard on screen and translate internal states into behavior, image, dialogue, or intentional voice channels.
rationale: Production-facing script action must be interpretable as screen events rather than inaccessible novelistic explanation.
when_to_use:
  - Writing scene actions for AI comic or screen adaptation.
  - Reviewing whether an emotion has a performable expression.
when_not_to_use:
  - To ban all voiceover, internal monologue, or subjective visual devices.
  - To prescribe camera direction before production requirements are known.
application_rules:
  - Replace abstract state labels with observable choice, gesture, spatial behavior, or speech when possible.
  - Keep internal narration only when it adds information or tension unavailable through action.
anti_patterns:
  - Action text states private thoughts with no visible or audible representation.
  - Adjectives such as powerful or devastated replace concrete behavior.
examples:
  - weak: She feels that no one can be trusted.
  - stronger: She accepts his key, then changes the access code while he watches.
source_reference:
  source_title: Screenplay Format
  source_author_or_organization: Matt Carless; BBC Writersroom

  publication_reference: BBC Writersroom screenplay-format guide, updated 2004-02-06.

  extraction_confidence: high
