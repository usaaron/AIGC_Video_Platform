---
knowledge_id: knowledge.character.desire_drives_action.v1
version: v1
category: character_design
principle: Give the focal character a concrete desire that directs action and exposes the costs they are willing to pay.
rationale: Desire converts a character profile into behavior and gives conflict a stable target.
when_to_use:
  - Establishing protagonist agency.
  - Testing whether scene goals belong to the same character trajectory.
when_not_to_use:
  - To reduce a complex character to one unchanging objective.
  - When uncertainty about desire is itself the explicit dramatic problem.
application_rules:
  - Phrase the desire as an attainable or resistible objective, not a personality adjective.
  - Show at least one action and one accepted risk linked to that desire.
anti_patterns:
  - Motivation is stated but never affects a choice.
  - Events move the protagonist while the protagonist pursues nothing.
examples:
  - weak: She is ambitious and receives an invitation.
  - stronger: She risks exposing her forged credentials to enter the meeting that can clear her name.
source_reference:
  source_title: The Anatomy of Story
  source_author_or_organization: John Truby

  publication_reference: Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2008, ISBN 9781429923705; Chapter 1 discussion of desire as a driver of action and cost.
  source_type: professional_screenwriting_book

  extraction_confidence: high
