category: genre_blueprint
principle: Treat dark romance as a porous romance mode in which intimacy is entangled with dangerous worlds, power imbalance, violence, taboo, or contested boundaries; do not reduce it to a fixed trope list.
genre_blueprint:
  audience_expectation: A central intimate relationship is meaningfully shaped by darkness, danger, taboo, or unequal power rather than merely decorated by them.
  emotional_promise: Intense and often ambivalent engagement with vulnerability, desire, danger, and power.
  common_story_engine: Attempts at intimacy alter power and risk, while the surrounding dark world makes trust and boundaries consequential.
  common_failure_modes: Mislabeling ordinary romance, aestheticizing harm without consequence, or treating a trope checklist as a definition.
  typical_conflict_patterns: Desire versus safety; intimacy versus control; consent and boundary negotiation; personal loyalty versus violent institutions.
rationale: Research describes the category as participant-defined, historically changing, and internally diverse, with darkness often operating through social power and threats around the central relationship.
when_to_use:
  - When the user explicitly requests dark romance and provides content boundaries.
  - Designing conflict where intimacy and danger affect one another.
when_not_to_use:
  - To relabel any romance with a cold or intimidating lead.
  - Without explicit safety, rating, consent, and avoid-pattern constraints.
application_rules:
  - Make the dark context alter trust, vulnerability, power, or choice in the relationship.
  - Preserve character motivation and consequence rather than aestheticizing harm without narrative function.
  - Record content warnings and user exclusions as constraints, not optional flavor.
anti_patterns:
  - Copying abuse, stalking, or coercion as a mandatory genre beat.
  - Treating violence alone as proof that the relationship is dramatically developed.
examples:
  - weak: A conventional romance adds arbitrary cruelty for branding.
  - stronger: The same institution that gives one lead power also makes genuine trust costly and forces both leads to negotiate boundaries.
