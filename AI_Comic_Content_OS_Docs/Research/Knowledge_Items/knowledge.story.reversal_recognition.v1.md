knowledge_id: knowledge.story.reversal_recognition.v1
version: v1
category: story_structure
principle: A strong reversal changes the direction of action, and a strong recognition changes what a character understands; the two are most effective when they arise from the established sequence of events.
rationale: Coupling changed circumstances with changed understanding can produce surprise that also feels earned.
when_to_use:
  - Designing a reveal that alters the protagonist's next choice.
  - Building an episode-ending turn from earlier evidence.
when_not_to_use:
  - As a mandatory twist in every scene or episode.
  - When recognition would only restate information already understood.
application_rules:
  - Identify the prior assumption and the evidence that overturns it.
  - Show how the new understanding changes action, relationship, or risk.
anti_patterns:
  - A twist changes facts but has no effect on behavior.
  - A recognition is delivered by exposition unrelated to prior action.
examples:
  - weak: A stranger announces the villain's identity at the end.
  - stronger: The protagonist recognizes that her own successful trap activated the villain's contingency.
