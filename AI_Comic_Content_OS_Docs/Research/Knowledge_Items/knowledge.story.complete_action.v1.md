---
knowledge_id: knowledge.story.complete_action.v1
version: v1
category: story_structure
principle: A dramatic unit should form a comprehensible whole whose beginning initiates movement, whose middle develops consequences, and whose ending resolves or redirects that movement.
rationale: Completeness helps the audience understand why events belong together rather than experiencing an arbitrary sequence.
when_to_use:
  - Planning a complete short episode or major dramatic unit.
  - Checking whether an ending follows from the action that preceded it.
when_not_to_use:
  - As a fixed three-act page-count formula.
  - To forbid open endings or serialized cliffhangers that still complete an episode-level movement.
application_rules:
  - Identify the initiating change, consequential development, and terminal state of the unit.
  - Require the ending to answer, transform, or intensify the dramatic question opened earlier.
anti_patterns:
  - A middle made of interchangeable incidents.
  - A cliffhanger unrelated to the episode's initiating action.
examples:
  - weak: A character investigates, then an unrelated villain appears only in the final shot.
  - stronger: The investigation exposes a witness, and the witness's reaction creates the final threat.
source_reference:
  source_title: Poetics
  source_author_or_organization: Aristotle; translated by S. H. Butcher
  source_url: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1974/1974-h/1974-h.htm
  publication_reference: Project Gutenberg eBook 1974; especially the discussion of a whole action in Part VII.
  source_type: foundational_dramatic_theory
  authority_level: foundational
  extraction_confidence: high
confidence: high
