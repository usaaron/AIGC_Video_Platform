version: v1
category: genre_blueprint
principle: Use a coherent speculative difference to estrange familiar reality and invite reasoned examination of its consequences, rather than using futuristic imagery as decoration alone.
genre_blueprint:
  audience_expectation: A speculative difference changes the represented world and supports intelligible consequences.
  emotional_promise: Wonder, unease, discovery, or critical reorientation toward familiar human and social questions.
  common_story_engine: A novum changes available choices, institutions, identity, or survival, and characters must reason and act within its consequences.
  common_failure_modes: Cosmetic futurism, arbitrary capabilities, unexplained resolution, or a premise that never affects behavior.
  typical_conflict_patterns: Human intention versus technological consequence; individual versus transformed institution; knowledge versus control; adaptation versus identity.
rationale: Suvin's influential account distinguishes science fiction through cognitively presented difference that changes how ordinary reality can be perceived.
when_to_use:
  - Science-fiction premises involving technology, institutions, environments, or alternate conditions.
  - Checking whether the speculative element drives conflict and choice.
when_not_to_use:
  - As the only valid definition of all science fiction.
  - To reject science fantasy or intentionally mythic speculative work.
application_rules:
  - State the central speculative departure and its governing assumptions.
  - Trace at least one social, personal, or moral consequence into character action.
  - Keep the premise internally legible even when not scientifically exhaustive.
anti_patterns:
  - Replace ordinary props with neon technology while preserving identical consequences.
  - Resolve conflict through an unprepared new capability.
examples:
  - weak: An AI exists only to deliver exposition.
  - stronger: The AI's ability to rewrite provenance changes what evidence, authorship, and trust mean for the engineer who built it.
source_reference:
