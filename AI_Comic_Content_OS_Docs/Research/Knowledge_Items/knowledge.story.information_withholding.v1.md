---
knowledge_id: knowledge.story.information_withholding.v1
version: v1
category: story_structure
principle: Reveal and withhold information deliberately so the audience has an active question to interpret, while retaining enough evidence for later understanding.
rationale: Managed information creates audience participation; indiscriminate concealment creates confusion rather than curiosity.
when_to_use:
  - Designing hooks, mystery beats, hidden motives, and cliffhangers.
  - Sequencing reveals in short episodes.
when_not_to_use:
  - To hide information a viewpoint character plainly knows solely to manufacture surprise.
  - When immediate clarity is required for emotional or safety context.
application_rules:
  - State the audience question associated with each withheld fact.
  - Plant observable evidence before or alongside delayed explanation.
anti_patterns:
  - A reveal depends on facts never available to the audience.
  - Every fact is withheld, leaving no stable dramatic question.
examples:
  - weak: The culprit is a new character introduced at the reveal.
  - stronger: Earlier behavior is visible but its motive is reinterpreted by the reveal.

  source_title: The Anatomy of Story
